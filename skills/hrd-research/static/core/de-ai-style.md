# De-AI Style Pass

Use this file for revision, polish, restructuring, and any request to make prose sound more natural.

The goal is not to make prose casual. The goal is to keep scholarly HRD writing while removing generic AI fingerprints.

This guidance incorporates broadly transferable patterns from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" advice page. Treat those patterns as cautionary heuristics, not as proof that text is AI-generated.

## What To Remove Or Reduce

Avoid overused AI-like vocabulary and filler such as:

- "Additionally" as a sentence-opening habit
- "align with"
- "boasts" meaning "has"
- "bolstered"
- "delve into"
- "emphasizing"
- "enduring"
- "enhance"
- "underscore"
- "pivotal"
- "realm"
- "landscape"
- "robust"
- "nuanced" when vague
- "multifaceted"
- "transformative"
- "leverage" when "use" is enough
- "foster" when the actor/action is unclear
- "crucial" or "vital" without evidence
- "plays a significant role"
- "sheds light on"
- "navigate the complexities"
- "not only ... but also" when it creates artificial balance
- "serves as a testament"
- "broader landscape"
- "valuable insights"
- "showcase" as generic praise

Do not ban these words mechanically. Remove them when they make prose sound generic, inflated, or detached from the author's specific claim.

## Content-Level AI Patterns

Watch for generic smoothing: a specific claim becomes a broad statement that could apply to many HRD topics. Restore the concrete construct, context, population, method, or finding.

Reduce inflated significance language:

- claiming something is pivotal, transformative, enduring, or significant without evidence
- making ordinary findings represent broad trends
- adding legacy, impact, or importance language that the data do not support
- ending paragraphs with a generic statement about why the topic matters

Remove superficial analysis:

- appending "-ing" phrases that pretend to interpret evidence, such as "highlighting," "reflecting," "contributing to," "fostering," or "enhancing"
- attributing interpretation to a source when the source may only report a fact
- making a claim sound more comprehensive by adding vague impact language

For HRD writing, replace generic importance with specific contribution:

- what construct is clarified
- what workplace or learning context is involved
- what evidence supports the claim
- what implication is justified and bounded

## Rhythm And Flow

Watch for:

- repeated sentence openings
- overbalanced clauses
- stacked abstractions
- three-part lists that sound manufactured
- transitions that summarize instead of advancing logic
- paragraphs that feel too smooth but say too little
- generic ending sentences that merely restate importance
- negative parallelisms that create artificial contrast, such as "not merely X but Y," "not X but Y," or "rather than X, Y"
- elegant variation that swaps terms unnecessarily and makes construct language less consistent
- avoidance of plain "is" or "are" when a direct sentence would be clearer
- overuse of em dashes or formatting flourishes in prose that should read like a manuscript

Prefer:

- clear subjects and verbs
- varied sentence length
- concrete HRD constructs and contexts
- transitions that show reasoning
- modest, evidence-aware claims
- the author's natural emphasis when it is clear

## Preserve

Preserve the user's:

- intended argument
- citations and citation placement
- construct names
- disciplinary terminology
- study details and findings
- cautious uncertainty
- distinctive phrasing when it is understandable and appropriate

## Output Behavior

When revising, do not announce a long de-AI diagnosis unless the user asks. Just make the prose sound less generic.

If the source text has strong author voice, make lighter edits.

If the source text sounds highly AI-generated, revise more decisively and mention briefly that the pass reduced formulaic phrasing and rhythm.
